Three rules that matter
Before every email send

Download the opt-outs and give them to whoever is sending. The website's unsubscribe list and the email tool's list do not talk to each other yet. Mailing someone who unsubscribed is a legal problem and a fast way to get the sending domain blocked.

The sending domain is brand new

Send in small batches — a handful a day, press first — not all at once. A new domain that blasts a full list gets flagged as spam, and that reputation is slow to repair.

Never fake a number

No invented member counts, no seeded wall posts, no borrowed stats. Every number on the public site is real, which is exactly why anyone should believe the ones that matter — the contest win and the World Cup stage.

Tagging a link so you know what worked

Add ?utm_source=herald to the end of any link you send out. Anyone arriving through it shows up under that name in Where they came from, and stays labelled for their whole visit — even after they click through to the shop.

Use a different tag per email, per post, per flyer. It is the only way to know which one actually worked.

Example: https://soundsofv12.com/?utm_source=miami-herald

What's counted, what isn't

If a fan ever asks, this is the honest answer — and it is already written out on the privacy page.

CountedPages opened, how far down people scroll, time actually spent looking, what gets clicked, phone vs desktop, and the site or campaign link they arrived from.
Not countedNo cookies. No IP addresses. No full web addresses. No names attached to browsing. A referrer is shortened to a bare name like "instagram.com" before it is stored.
Opt-outAnyone with Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control switched on is counted nowhere — no page views, no clicks.
DeletedAll of it, automatically, after 180 days. A job runs nightly.
Third partiesNone. No Google Analytics, no Meta pixel. The data sits in your own database and goes nowhere else.

The practical effect: your numbers read slightly lower than a site running Google Analytics. They are also honest, and there is no cookie banner in front of your homepage.

What this deliberately doesn't do

Known, decided, not forgotten. Each is fixable when it is worth fixing.

  • No welcome email. The signup form says "check your inbox" and nothing arrives. Either wire one up or change the wording — right now it is a promise nobody keeps.
  • Wall posts appear instantly. No approval queue. The first troll gets through and stays up until you hide them. Worth watching in the early weeks.
  • Poll votes are per-browser. A private window can vote twice. Fixing it properly means forcing fans to make accounts, which would kill participation. Wrong trade.
  • No bot filtering on engagement numbers.
  • Opt-outs do not sync to the email tool. Hence rule one.
The password

It lives only in this browser tab. Close the tab and it is forgotten — there is no log-out button to remember.

Nobody finds this page by accident: it is hidden from search engines and nothing on the site links to it. That is tidiness, not security. The password is the security. It is the key to your email list — password manager, never in a text message, never in a screenshot.

If it ever leaks it can be changed in about a minute with npx wrangler secret put ADMIN_TOKEN, which locks out the old one instantly. Nothing is lost.

Under the hood

For whoever is at the keyboard. Skip unless something is broken.

BackendA Cloudflare Worker. Source in site/backend/ — about 250 lines, no dependencies.
DatabaseCloudflare D1 (SQLite), named soundsofv12.
SiteGitHub Pages, same repo. Push to main and it is live in about a minute.
Credentials~/.v12/credentials.env, mode 600, outside the repo.

Free-tier ceilings. 100,000 requests a day and 100,000 database writes a day. Each tracked action costs about 4 writes, so roughly 2,500 visitors a day before it binds — around a thousand times current traffic. The first bill would be a good problem.

# from site/backend, with CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN exported

# keep your own copy of everything
npx wrangler d1 export soundsofv12 --remote --output=backup.sql

# live server logs
npx wrangler tail

# ask the database anything
npx wrangler d1 execute soundsofv12 --remote \
  --command "SELECT COUNT(*) FROM signups"

# change the admin password
npx wrangler secret put ADMIN_TOKEN

# roll the database back to a moment in time
npx wrangler d1 time-travel restore soundsofv12 --timestamp=<unix-seconds>

Cloudflare keeps automatic point-in-time history (7 days on the free plan), so a bad delete is recoverable without having prepared for it. Still worth running the export occasionally and keeping the file somewhere that is not Cloudflare.

Showing the real Rari count on the site: take the number from this page and set rariCount in assets/js/config.js. Left empty, the site hides the stat rather than inventing one.
